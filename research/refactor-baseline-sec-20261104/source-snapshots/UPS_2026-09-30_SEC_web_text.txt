L18: ATLANTA – October 27, 2026 – UPS (NYSE:UPS) today announced third-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue of $22.9 billion. Consolidated operating profit was $1.7 billion; non-GAAP adjusted consolidated operating profit was $2.1 billion. Diluted earnings per share were $1.37; non-GAAP adjusted diluted earnings per share were $1.69†.
L19: For the third quarter of 2026, GAAP results include a net charge of $279 million, or $0.32 per diluted share, comprised of after-tax transformation strategy costs of $130 million, as well as a $182 million charge associated with a goodwill impairment in Supply Chain Solutions, partially offset by a gain on sale of MD-11 assets of $33 million.
L20: “I want to thank all UPSers for their unwavering commitment to our customers and for delivering another quarter of strong performance,” said Carol Tomé, UPS chief executive officer. “Our third-quarter results demonstrate the continued strengthening of our business fundamentals, driven by premium growth and greater network efficiency. As momentum builds, we are entering the holiday shipping season from a position of strength, with the most automated and efficient network in our history.
L21: Given the strength we see in the business, we are raising our full-year financial guidance.”

L117: Transformation Strategy Costs
L118: We exclude the impact of charges related to initiatives within our transformation strategy. Our transformation strategy initiatives have spanned several years and are designed to fundamentally change the spans and layers of our organization structure, processes, technologies and the composition of our business portfolio.
L119: Various circumstances precipitated these initiatives, including identification and prioritization of certain investments, developments and changes in competitive landscapes, inflationary pressures, consumer behaviors, and other factors including post-COVID normalization and volume diversions attributed to our 2023 labor negotiations.
L120: 
L121: Our transformation strategy has included the following initiatives:
L122: Transformation 2.0: We reduced spans and layers of management, reviewed and refined our business portfolio and invested in certain technologies to reduce costs, increase visibility and reduce reliance on legacy systems. Costs associated with Transformation 2.0 consisted primarily of compensation and benefit costs related to reductions in our workforce and fees paid to third-party consultants. This initiative was completed in 2025.
L123: Fit to Serve: We undertook our Fit to Serve initiative to right-size our business to create a more efficient operating model that was more responsive to market dynamics through a workforce reduction, primarily within management. This initiative was completed in 2025.
L124: Network Reconfiguration and Efficiency Reimagined: Our Network of the Future initiative is enhancing the efficiency of our U.S. Domestic Package network through automation and operational sort consolidation.
L125: In connection with our strategic execution of planned volume declines from our largest customer, we began our Network Reconfiguration initiative in the fourth quarter of 2024, which was an expansion of Network of the Future and under which we are further consolidating facilities, vehicles, aircraft and our workforce, as well as, executing an end-to-end process redesign through our Efficiency Reimagined  initiative.
L126: * * *
L127: 
L128: Through these initiatives, in the first nine months of 2026, we reduced our operational workforce, closed certain daily operations at leased and owned buildings and achieved approximately $1.8 billion of program benefits. We expect to achieve nearly $3 billion in full year 2026 benefits from these initiatives.
L129: As a part of these initiatives, we expect non-GAAP adjusted operating expense to exclude between $1.4 and $1.6 billion in cost for the full year 2026, primarily related to employee separation costs and third-party consulting fees, of which $1.2 billion is related to the Driver Choice Program. As of September 30, 2026 we had incurred $1.4 billion in costs during 2026, and $1.9 billion in total costs, as a part of these initiatives.
L130: While the planned volume reductions from our largest customer were completed in the second quarter of 2026, our Efficiency Reimagined initiative is not expected to conclude until the fourth quarter of 2027.
L131: We do not consider the related costs to be ordinary because each program involves separate and distinct activities that span multiple periods, and such costs are not expected to drive incremental revenue. These initiatives exceed ordinary, ongoing efforts to enhance our business performance and profitability.
